# Planners demand programmes that survive the contract

Across Asta, P6, MS Project and the newer platforms, the strongest and most independent demand is not for more features. Planners want a programme that holds up contractually. That means clean XER, MPP and Asta exchange, because clients name the format in the NEC4 Scope. It means a CPM engine they can trust with multiple calendars and out-of-sequence progress. And it means a programme that passes NEC4 acceptance first time. Behind those sit three pains UK planners raise whatever tool they use. Site progress arrives as WhatsApp messages and spreadsheets that someone re-keys. Hours go into submission packs: a PDF, a change narrative, and a comparison with the last Accepted Programme. Incumbent tools are hard to learn and come with licensing friction. **Our product already covers the engine side well.** We have longest path, several link types between the same pair, multiple calendars, DCMA 14 with auto-repair, named baselines, Monte Carlo, browser access and an MCP server. MS Project, Smartsheet and Procore Scheduling users say their tools lack exactly these. **The biggest gaps against evidenced demand are output and evidence-trail features.** We lack print/PDF with title blocks, programme comparison with generated narratives, a clause 31.2 checker, multiple progress periods to build an as-built record, codes, user-defined fields and saved views, and multi-user working. AI demand is real but conditional. Construction PMs use general chatbots far more than embedded vendor AI (ChatGPT 66.7% against 3.7%) and name trust as the top barrier ([Mastt](https://www.mastt.com/research/ai-in-construction-project-management-2026)). That favours our MCP route and an AI that proposes changes rather than applying them. The evidence is thin. Review sites and vendor idea portals were blocked. Frequency signals count independent sources, not votes. Much of the UK material comes from consultancies that sell NEC programme services.

## Evidence comes from forums, trainers and vendors, not votes

Read every frequency signal in this report as a lower bound from a thin sample. Capterra, G2, Software Advice, GetApp and Gartner Peer Insights returned HTTP 403, so review evidence is search-engine snippets, not full reviews. TrustRadius returned 404. Reddit was blocked to the fetch tool, and LinkedIn posts were not indexed. Interoperability ranks first because the contract creates the demand, not taste. NEC4 lets the Scope fix the software and format. Clients and PMs want native files so they can interrogate logic and float ([NEC Planning Solutions](https://www.necplanningsolutions.co.uk/post/what-project-manager-checks-nec-programme-review) [C]). In UK practice, the client's mandate decides the tool more than features do. A Planning Planet commenter notes that most planners have no authority to choose their tools ([Planning Planet](https://planningplanet.com/forums/planning-scheduling-programming-discussion/561014/primavera-p6-and-powerproject)). Vendors' own releases confirm the pressure. Elecosoft's autumn 2026 Asta release adds **native Primavera P6 interoperability add-ins** ([PBC Today](https://www.pbctoday.co.uk/news/?p=166904) [V]). Procore was still shipping an "improved XER import" in July 2026, five months after general availability ([Procore release notes](https://support.procore.com/products/online/user-guide/project-level/scheduling/release-notes) [V]). One trap is specific to us. MS Project allows **only one relationship between two activities** ([Eastwood Harris](https://eastwoodharris.com/comparison-of-microsoft-project-oracle-primavera-p6-and-elecosoft-asta-powerproject/)) and cannot process duplicate predecessor relationships ([Ten Six](https://tensix.com/exporting-from-primavera-p6-to-microsoft-project/)). Our support for several links per pair is therefore an engine strength that becomes an export risk unless MSPDI export drops the extra links in a controlled way and says so.

On the engine, the evidence favours us. The most specific construction complaints about MS Project all concern correctness rather than the interface: no longest path, Total Slack made "unintelligible" by multiple calendars, deadlines and levelling, and one hours-per-day constant across every calendar ([projectcontrols](https://projectcontrols.com/comment/88396) [O]). A Microsoft MVP says there is "functionally no difference" between Project 2019 and 2021, so these flaws will stay ([Tech Community](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/discussions/project/ms-project-software-update/4055157)). The newer platforms are weaker still. Procore Scheduling reached general availability in February 2026 without baselines or custom workday calendars, and its announcement does not mention critical path ([Procore](https://www.procore.com/blog/procore-scheduling-reaches-general-availability) [V]). Among P6 experts, the demand is for **guardrails and transparency more than new algorithms**. Oracle added an optional out-of-sequence alert only in 25.12. It does not fire for Apply Actuals or Update Progress ([Ten Six](https://tensix.com/p6-professional-25-12-is-released/)). Retained logic "generally gives the longest critical path", while override can make the project look more optimistic ([P6 Consulting](https://www.p6consulting.ca/blog/progress-override-vs-retained-logic-which-to-use-and-what-to-look-out-for/)).

The NEC evidence is the most UK-specific in the corpus and also the most consultancy-dependent, so it needs careful reading. The most authoritative voice is a construction-law consultant with over 20 years on NEC. He has "**still never found a programme that is fully compliant with the contract**". The site loop is the oldest pain in the corpus and still unsolved. HKA's observation that the contractor often measures progress "differently from the engineer/consultant", causing "confusion, extra effort and subsequently extra cost", appears alongside UK academic findings that **lack of contemporaneous records is the key obstacle to early delay assessment** ([HKA](https://www.hka.com/article/project-records-from-a-delay-analysis-perspective/); [Buildings](https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings3030598)). Even the largest platform shipped without closing this loop. Procore lists mobile field updates that sync back to the schedule as a 2026 roadmap item, not a launch feature ([Procore](https://www.procore.com/blog/procore-scheduling-reaches-general-availability) [V]). Our look-ahead is derived from the master programme, which matches UK advice that the weekly look-ahead should be "cut from the programme rather than drawn beside it" ([forgecommand](https://forgecommand.co.uk/blog/construction-programme-management) [V]). But we keep only one progress state. Without multiple progress periods, our product cannot yet build the chain of updates that commercial teams and delay experts rely on. In *Walter Lilly v Mackay* both experts accepted that the absence of a contemporaneous critical-path programme was an underlying problem ([schedulelens](https://schedulelens.com/blog/prospective-vs-retrospective-delay-analysis/) [V]).

Vendors' releases point to baselines and comparison as a growing area of demand. Oracle's P6 25.12 contained **five separate baseline items** ([Plan Academy](https://www.planacademy.com/whats-new-in-primavera-p6-version-25-12/)). Asta's 2026.3 release headlines "intelligent baseline and revision management" ([PBC Today](https://www.pbctoday.co.uk/news/?p=166904) [V]). Primavera Cloud keeps adding baseline-variance filters ([Equivtech 26.9](https://www.equivtech.com.au/Primavera-Cloud-New-Update-26-9.html) [V]). We already match the baseline half. The comparison half, a diff of logic, dates, float and time risk allowances against the last accepted programme, is unbuilt everywhere in the evidence and is assembled by hand today.

The AI evidence is clearer about what planners won't accept than about what they want. The RICS survey, the best independent source with a UK weighting, finds interest without adoption. Under 1% of organisations have AI fully embedded, and **44% of PMs worry about AI's effect on their own role** ([RICS](https://www.rics.org/news-insights/artificial-intelligence-in-construction-report)). Mastt's smaller vendor-run 2026 survey shows that the AI people do use is general-purpose. That supports exposing our engine through MCP rather than building a separate AI app. Competitors are moving the same way, so MCP alone will not stay a differentiator for long. Liability commentary from the UK and Canada puts responsibility on whoever submits an unchecked AI programme. That suggests what to build: AI and auto-repair that show a diff, explain each change and leave the planner to accept it.

Risk and resources are weakly evidenced in this corpus, and our product already covers them strongly. Treat them as strengths to demonstrate, not gaps to fill. The one specifically UK signal is that time risk allowances must be visible and separate from float under NEC. That belongs with the clause 31.2 checker rather than the Monte Carlo module.

Several of our "not yet" items have **little or no evidence in this corpus**: cash flow, split tasks (one Asta reviewer asked for suspend/resume), annotations, and multiple tasks per bar line. That is a gap in the evidence, not proof of low demand. The research found no Asta printing or presentation complaints, and those features are where Asta's users would notice their absence. Leaving BIM and 4D out of scope is consistent with the evidence. BIM and design rank low as an AI priority, and generative tools stall on BIM input requirements ([Mastt](https://www.mastt.com/research/ai-in-construction-project-management-2026) [V]; [AI Home Building](https://aihomebuilding.com/articles/ai-scheduling-garbage-data)).

## Conclusion

The market's unmet need is less a better Gantt than an **evidence layer around the programme**: what was submitted, what changed, what site reported, and which version was accepted. Incumbents are converging on pieces of it: Asta Vision's governance and revision management, Primavera Cloud's central health checks, and Procore's links between RFIs and submittals. Each sits behind a paid tier or inside one vendor's platform, and none ties those pieces to NEC4's contractual mechanics. Our engine already outclasses the tools many UK teams use for day-to-day planning. The risk is commercial rather than technical. If the XER that reaches P6 is imperfect, the PDF looks worse than Asta's, or the submission pack still has to be assembled by hand, the product will be judged on its outputs before anyone sees the engine.

The evidence base cannot settle priorities on its own. It is snippet-level, contains no votes, leans on consultancies and vendors, and includes almost no first-hand UK planner voice from after 2020. The cheapest next step is direct validation. Ask perhaps ten UK planners and NEC PMs to rank the clause 31.2 checker, the version comparison with a narrative, and print/PDF against each other. Ask them to name the XER round-trip failures that have actually cost them a submission.
