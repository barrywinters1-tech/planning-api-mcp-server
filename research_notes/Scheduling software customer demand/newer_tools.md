# Customer demand and complaints: newer and adjacent construction scheduling tools (Procore, Smartsheet, nPlan, ALICE, Tilos, Synchro, Buildertrend, Fieldwire/Outbuild/Touchplan, AI entrants)

Method note for the report writer: 17 tool calls (web search plus fetch). Direct fetches of Capterra review pages were blocked (HTTP 403), and G2 review text came only as search-engine excerpts, so review quotes below are excerpts as surfaced by search and were not read in full on the page. Reddit-specific searches returned no relevant threads, so there is **no Reddit evidence** in these notes. Much of the comparison content online comes from competing vendors (ProjectManager.com, Planera, Onplana, Outbuild, GetOneCrew). These are labelled as such and are weaker evidence than user reviews. Frequency signal key: **High** = repeated across 3+ independent sources or review sites; **Medium** = 2 sources; **Low** = single source or vendor-only.

## 1. What do users of these tools say they still lack for real CPM programme work (logic, critical path, baselines, XER/MPP interop, printing)?

### Takeaway
The pattern is consistent across the newer and adjacent tools. Procore, Smartsheet, Buildertrend, Fieldwire and Touchplan are seen as viewers, collaboration layers or simple Gantt tools. Users say none of them replaces P6 or MS Project for the contract or master programme. The gaps users name most often are weak or absent CPM calculation (float, constraints, full relationship types), shallow baselines and variance, and one-way, read-only or lossy XER/MPP interop. The result is that teams run two tools and re-key data. Printing and layout output barely came up in the evidence I found (gap).

### Cited Findings
**Procore (Schedule tool and the new Procore Scheduling): frequency High for "viewer, not a CPM tool"**
- G2 reviewer excerpt: Procore's scheduling tool "is mostly a static viewer." — [G2 Procore pros and cons](https://www.g2.com/products/procore/reviews?qs=pros-and-cons) (search excerpt, undated)
- G2 reviewer excerpt: "Currently, the Schedule tool primarily supports Microsoft Project file formats, which limits the ability to incorporate other valuable project-planning resources." — [G2 Procore reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/procore/reviews) (search excerpt)
- G2 reviewer excerpt: "importing/exporting data between Procore and external scheduling tools (like Primavera) can be cumbersome." — [G2 Procore reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/procore/reviews) (search excerpt)
- Procore Scheduling reached general availability on 17 Feb 2026. The GA post emphasises assigning responsibility, 360 Reporting, APIs, internationalisation and P6/MS Project import. It does **not** mention CPM, critical path, lookahead or AI. Version history, baseline comparisons, custom workday calendars and mobile field updates are listed as spring/summer 2026 roadmap items, so baselines and calendars were missing at GA (vendor source). — [Procore blog, 19 Feb 2026](https://www.procore.com/blog/procore-scheduling-reaches-general-availability)
- Procore's user guide says GCs import master schedules as **read-only records** and build lookaheads in Procore (vendor documentation, via search summary). — [Procore support: Scheduling (Project)](https://support.procore.com/products/online/user-guide/project-level/scheduling)
- Release notes list an "improved XER import" on 21 Jul 2026 that handles successive Oracle Primavera Cloud exports. This shows XER interop is still being fixed after GA (vendor). — [Procore Scheduling release notes](https://support.procore.com/products/online/user-guide/project-level/scheduling/release-notes)
- Competitor claims (Planera, a CPM scheduler with a Procore integration): Procore re-import "overwrites everything" while Planera merges, and "Activity IDs are stripped on P6 import." Another competitor comparison (Feb 2026) says Procore Scheduling does no forward/backward pass and no automated float calculation. Treat all three as competitor claims that I could not verify. — [Planera: Procore alternatives](https://www.planera.io/post/procore-alternatives) (my direct fetch of this page did *not* show the overwrite or Activity ID claims, so they may sit on Planera's separate "Planera vs Procore Scheduling" page)
- Comparison site: Procore's Schedule tool lacks "a native CPM engine, algorithmic resource leveling, an EVM module, progress override methods, or DCMA-14 quality checks", lookaheads are "capped at six weeks", and owners and sureties "expect schedule files in XER format... a Gantt visualizer cannot produce that." — attributed in search summary to [whatisbest.com: Procore vs Primavera](https://www.whatisbest.com/construction/procore-vs-oracle-primavera-which-is-better-for-enterprise-project-scheduling) (third-party, not verified on page)
- ProjectManager.com (a competitor) says advanced resource leveling, variance analysis and critical path depth require importing from MS Project or P6. — [ProjectManager: Procore for PM](https://www.projectmanager.com/blog/procore-project-management)

**Smartsheet: frequency High for "no true CPM"; specifics disputed**
- Scheduling consultancy (Mustardseed PMO): Smartsheet calculates critical path only against the project's latest end date, not intermediate milestones. It has no task constraints such as "must start on", which limits negative-float analysis. Float and critical path on parent summary rows are unreliable. When identical tasks exist, only one may show as critical. — [Mustardseed PMO](https://www.mustardseedpmo.com/project-management-insight-page/understanding-smartsheets-approach-to-float-and-critical-path-method)
- Onplana (a competitor) says Smartsheet does not calculate float or critical path and its links are finish-to-start only. ProjectManager (also a competitor) says Smartsheet supports all four relationship types with lag and highlights the critical path on Pro and above. These two sources conflict. — [Onplana vs Smartsheet](https://onplana.com/blog/onplana-vs-smartsheet); [ProjectManager: Smartsheet alternatives](https://www.projectmanager.com/blog/smartsheet-alternatives-construction); [ProjectManager: Smartsheet for GCs](https://www.projectmanager.com/blog/smartsheet-for-general-contractors-key-features-pros-cons)
- Capterra user reviews mention limited options for customising task dependencies in Gantt view. — [Capterra Smartsheet reviews p5](https://www.capterra.com/p/79104/9teams/reviews/?page=5) (search excerpt)
- Heavy sheets slow down, and per-user pricing adds up for large teams. — [Buildern review](https://buildern.com/resources/blog/construction-scheduling-software/)

**Buildertrend (residential): frequency Medium-High for "too simple for complex or commercial work"**
- 7 Apr 2026 reviewer (as quoted by a third party): scheduling "works but can be very simple, and won't allow the complexity needed on larger commercial projects." — [Fervor Studio Buildertrend review](https://fervorstudio.ca/news/buildertrend-review-pricing-alternatives/)
- Users report dependency set-up mistakes that make tasks move unexpectedly, and a confusing set-up for which one user asked for a manual. — [Software Finder Buildertrend reviews](https://softwarefinder.com/construction/buildertrend-software/reviews); [FindPMSoftware](https://findpmsoftware.com/products/buildertrend/reviews)
- ProjectManager (competitor): only basic task dependencies and basic baseline and variance tracking. — [ProjectManager: best construction scheduling software](https://www.projectmanager.com/blog/best-construction-scheduling-software)
- GetOneCrew (competitor) summarises the most common complaints as a steep learning curve, scheduling too simple for commercial work, limited integrations, and price rises at renewal. — [GetOneCrew](https://www.getonecrew.com/post/buildertrend-reviews)

**Fieldwire / Touchplan / Outbuild: frequency Medium for "not a master-schedule tool"**
- Fieldwire scheduling is task-based, not CPM. It imports MS Project and P6 but does not advertise pushing progress back to the master schedule, and it is not built for master scheduling or resource loading (vendor-authored comparisons, via search summary). Ratings are about 4.5 from 405 reviews. — [Guideflow comparison](https://www.guideflow.com/blog/construction-scheduling-software); [TechnBrains](https://www.technbrains.com/construction-software-development/scheduling-software/)
- Touchplan is planning-focused rather than a full CPM master schedule, so teams commonly run a second tool. Some reviewers report schedule readability problems. G2 rating is about 4.3 from 199 reviews. — [Guideflow](https://www.guideflow.com/blog/construction-scheduling-software); [Gitnux lean software](https://gitnux.org/best/lean-construction-software/)
- Outbuild Capterra reviewers (search excerpts): tying predecessors and successors in the lookahead "gets complicated". One reviewer wants new lookahead tasks to update the master schedule instead of switching between the two views. Others mention limited output customisation. — [Capterra Outbuild reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/174472/Outbuild/reviews/) (page itself returned 403; search excerpt only)
- Common industry pattern described in comparisons: keep P6 for owner-required, cost-loaded submittals and use Touchplan or Outbuild for weekly and lookahead work. — [Outbuild blog (vendor)](https://www.outbuild.com/blog/best-construction-scheduling-software); [Constructioncoverage](https://constructioncoverage.com/construction-scheduling-software)

**Synchro (scheduling side) and Tilos: frequency Low (small review counts)**
- Synchro: reviewers describe a steep learning curve ("a lot of tools... overwhelming", "very difficult to become completely familiar with for the occasional user") and few tutorials. One user notes it cannot import SureTrak schedules. Reviewers praise it as "the most visual scheduling software" and more to the point than Primavera. — [Capterra Synchro reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/35289/Synchro/reviews/); [Software Advice Synchro](https://www.softwareadvice.com/product/6302-Synchro/)
- Tilos: the UI "looks and feels antiquated". Reviewers cite poor integration with other tools and limited customisation. They praise its time-location diagrams for rail, pipelines and roads, and one ex-Primavera user said CPM "is not suitable at all" for their linear work. About 10 reviews, rated 4.1, some dating from 2019 (older evidence). — [SoftwareFinder Tilos](https://softwarefinder.com/project-management-software/tilos/reviews); [Capterra Tilos](https://www.capterra.com/p/235928/TILOS/)

### Inferences
- Unmet demand: a tool that combines a real CPM engine (full relationship types, lags, constraints, multiple calendars, float, longest path) with lossless round-trip XER/MPP that keeps activity IDs, merges on re-import and supports export back to XER for owners and sureties. Interop that works one way only (read-only import) is the most-named friction point for every collaboration-first tool.
- Even Procore, with the most distribution, shipped GA without baselines, calendars or mobile field updates. Incumbent-grade CPM functions (baselines, calendars, XER out) are therefore still a moat that the newer platforms have not crossed.
- Owners' contractual XER requirements are the main structural reason P6 survives. Any new product must accept P6 as the deliverable format rather than try to displace it.
- Linear and time-location scheduling (Tilos) is a niche where CPM-only tools fail, and Tilos's dated UI suggests the niche is under-served.

### Gaps
- Printing, plotting and layout complaints for these tools: no evidence found.
- Full G2/Capterra review text could not be retrieved (403), so frequency counts are qualitative, not counted.
- GanttPRO and LetsBuild: no construction-specific user feedback found in this pass.
- Whether Procore Scheduling's 2026 roadmap items (baselines, calendars) have shipped since summer 2026 is unconfirmed.

## 2. What site-team collaboration and progress-updating features do users value or ask for (mobile updates, look-ahead/last planner, photos, constraints logs, field-to-programme feedback)?

### Takeaway
Users value tools that bring the master programme to the field: lookaheads, Last Planner pull planning, assigning responsibility, and mobile progress updates. The recurring ask is a two-way link in which field and lookahead updates flow back into the master CPM programme without re-keying. Today most tools treat the master as a read-only import, and progress stops at the lookahead layer.

### Cited Findings
- Outbuild reviewer request: edit the main schedule when tasks are added in the lookahead instead of switching views. This is field-to-programme feedback demand (frequency Low-Medium). — [Capterra Outbuild reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/174472/Outbuild/reviews/) (search excerpt)
- Outbuild positions itself as strongest "when the gap is between the master schedule and the field" (vendor). A third-party guide says it is designed for lookahead planning and Last Planner workflows. — [Outbuild blog](https://www.outbuild.com/blog/best-construction-scheduling-software); [Planera: Outbuild alternatives (competitor)](https://www.planera.io/post/outbuild-alternatives)
- Touchplan is consistently described as purpose-built for Last Planner pull planning and syncs with P6 and MS Project master schedules. — [Constructioncoverage](https://constructioncoverage.com/construction-scheduling-software); [Outbuild blog](https://www.outbuild.com/blog/best-construction-scheduling-software)
- Procore says mobile field progress, date and note updates that sync to desktop are on the 2026 **roadmap**, not in GA. This means even the largest construction platform shipped without field-to-schedule updating (vendor). Procore's own beta metrics claim active projects grew more than 14x and active users more than 1,100% during beta, which signals demand for a schedule inside the field platform (vendor claim). — [Procore GA blog](https://www.procore.com/blog/procore-scheduling-reaches-general-availability)
- Procore's planned integrations: Submittals linked to schedule activities for date-conflict visibility, with RFIs, Inspections and Action Plans to follow. These are constraints-log-style links between project records and activities (vendor roadmap). — [Procore platform enhancements](https://www.procore.com/en-ae/blog/procore-shapes-future-of-construction-with-new-platform-enhancements)
- Planera markets an "RFI-to-Schedule Risk Scanner" in Procore AI that links open RFIs to matching activities and explicitly "never changes dates, durations, or logic." It also markets pushing logic, WBS, critical path, percent complete, actuals and baselines into Procore. This shows vendors are monetising the master-to-field link (vendor claim). — [Planera: Procore alternatives](https://www.planera.io/post/procore-alternatives)
- With manual uploads, "the schedule in Procore is only as current as the last upload", which is the stale-schedule problem (competitor framing). — [Planera](https://www.planera.io/post/procore-alternatives)
- Fieldwire is valued for task, plan and photo field management but does not push progress back to the master. — [Guideflow](https://www.guideflow.com/blog/construction-scheduling-software)

### Inferences
- The highest-value collaboration gap is closing the loop. Field percent-complete, actuals and constraint status (RFIs, submittals, materials) should feed back to the CPM programme as proposed updates a planner can review and accept, not as blind overwrites. Planera's "never changes logic" wording suggests planners want AI and field inputs to be advisory, with the planner in control.
- Linking constraints (RFI, submittal, inspection) to activities is the feature vendors are converging on in 2026. A planning API or MCP server could expose this generically (constraint objects linked to activity IDs) rather than through one vendor's platform.

### Gaps
- No direct practitioner (Lean Construction Institute, Reddit, LinkedIn) discussion of Last Planner tooling pain was retrieved. Searches returned only vendor comparison pages.
- No user evidence on photos or progress-capture-to-schedule (for example Buildots or Doxel) was gathered in this pass.

## 3. What AI capabilities do construction schedulers actually want or distrust (auto-generation, risk forecasting, schedule QA, natural-language edits), and what evidence of real adoption exists?

### Takeaway
Interest in AI is high but actual use for scheduling is low. Trust and accuracy is the number-one barrier. Practitioners mostly use general-purpose chatbots, not construction-specific AI. Specialist AI scheduling tools (nPlan for risk forecasting, ALICE for generative sequencing) have vendor case studies, but I found no independent audits of their claimed savings. Planners want AI that advises (risk flags, QA, linking RFIs to activities) rather than AI that silently changes logic.

### Cited Findings
- Mastt "State of AI in Construction PM 2026" (108 respondents, fieldwork Mar–Jun 2026, published 23 Jul 2026): **"Accuracy and trust is the #1 barrier" at 29.6%**, then understanding how to use AI 15.7%, tool integration 13.9%, skills gap 13.0%, cost only 3.7%. 62.0% see AI adding value in scheduling. Desired builds: reporting automation about 25%, cost and schedule integration about 10%, agentic AI about 6%, BIM/design about 3%, skeptical or unsure about 9%. Tools used in the last 3 months: ChatGPT 66.7%, Copilot 50.9%, Claude 34.3%, Gemini 30.6%, AI inside Procore/Autodesk **3.7%**, specialist AI **0.9%**. AI agents: 9.3% use, 46.3% plan to, and 21.3% "know them but won't hand over tasks". Small sample from a vendor (Mastt), but the most current data found. — [Mastt 2026 report](https://www.mastt.com/research/ai-in-construction-project-management-2026)
- Secondary roundup attributing to ConstructionOwners.com: about 16% of contractors use AI or automation for scheduling, and 60% have no plans to. "Only 12% of baseline schedules meet high-quality standards." The original source is not verified. — [Bridgit AI construction stats](https://gobridgit.com/blog/ai-construction-statistics/)
- RICS 2025 (2,200+ professionals): 45% of organisations report no AI use and 1% have scaled AI across projects, while about 70% of PMs and QSs believe AI will add value (via search summary). — [AI PropTech News](https://aiproptechnews.com/article/optimism-grows-for-ai-in-construction-despite-adoption-challenges/)
- Slate Technologies survey: 74% see value, only 13% plan adoption (vendor survey). — [Slate 2025 report](https://slate.ai/ai-construction-2025-industry-report/)
- Critical commentary: ALICE duration-saving claims are self-reported with no third-party audit. nPlan's "$1.2bn savings" claim lacks a published methodology. ALICE requires BIM or structured parametric inputs that many organisations cannot supply. AI tools only help once the underlying schedules are sound ("88% of construction schedules are garbage"). This is an independent-looking blog with opinion weight only. — [AI Home Building](https://aihomebuilding.com/articles/ai-scheduling-garbage-data)
- ALICE's own positioning: AI handles routine work and does not replace scheduler judgment, and it must "live alongside a mandated P6 deliverable, not replace it" (vendor). — [ALICE blog](https://blog.alicetechnologies.com/what-ai-construction-scheduling-actually-does-for-schedulers-and-project-controls-teams)
- Some feedback mentions a learning curve for ALICE. — [ai-review.com ALICE](https://ai-review.com/business/alice-technologies/); [teachustechnology](https://teachustechnology.com/alice-technologies-ai-construction-scheduling/)
- Buyer framing: nPlan suits predicting where delays occur from historical precedent (risk on existing schedules), and ALICE suits generating alternative sequences. — [BestCRE ALICE review](https://bestcre.com/alice-technologies-review-cre-ai/); [BuiltStack ALICE vs nPlan](https://getbuiltstack.com/guides/alice-technologies-vs-nplan/)
- nPlan adoption evidence (all vendor case studies): Network Rail used it on the Great Western Main Line. On the Transpennine Route Upgrade (2024), a six-month period produced "80 readiness for possessions risk analyses, generating 6,000 risk insights" and is claimed to have helped avoid £5m in cost. TRU "intends to use nPlan instead of QSRA" programme-wide after a gradual introductory phase. Other named users are Shell and Skanska. The vendor claims "30+ companies". — [nPlan TRU case study PDF](https://cdn.featuredcustomers.com/CustomerCaseStudy.document/nplan_transpennine-route-upgrade-tru-west_941193.pdf); [nPlan Network Rail case study](https://cdn.featuredcustomers.com/CustomerCaseStudy.document/nplan_network-rail2_941196.pdf); [AEC Hub nPlan](https://www.aechub.org/tools/nplan)
- Planera markets an AI Scheduling Assistant and a "Planera MCP connector for Claude and ChatGPT", which shows natural-language and LLM access to CPM schedules being productised by a 2025-funded entrant (vendor claim). — [Planera](https://www.planera.io/post/procore-alternatives)
- Procore tied its Helix AI to scheduling in roadmap language, but a competitor notes there was no schedule intelligence in the shipped GA product. I found no Helix scheduling feature announcement. — [Procore enhancements](https://www.procore.com/en-ae/blog/procore-shapes-future-of-construction-with-new-platform-enhancements)
- New entrants and funding, 2025: Planera raised $8M in October 2025, focused on data-centre CPM scheduling. — [Planera funding post](https://www.planera.io/post/planera-raises-8m-data-center-construction-scheduling). LeanCon raised a $6M seed for AI pre-construction planning that generates cost, schedule, logistics and method projections. — [Calcalist](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/s1ho14iw11e). Trunk Tools raised $40M for construction AI agents, including scheduling and tracking admin. — [Yahoo Finance / BI](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/startup-says-made-chatgpt-construction-130001710.html). A New Haven start-up raised $6M for AI-driven construction planning. — [Hartford Business](https://hartfordbusiness.com/article/new-haven-startup-raises-6m-for-ai-driven-construction-planning/). About 68% of $3.96bn of Q2 2025 built-environment tech investment went to AI/ML companies (secondary). — [Landbase](https://www.landbase.com/blog/fastest-growing-construction-tech-companies)

### Inferences
- Demand signal: planners already use general LLMs (ChatGPT, Copilot, Claude), not embedded vendor AI (3.7%) or specialist AI (0.9%). An LLM-accessible interface to the planner's own CPM data (an MCP or API layer) fits existing behaviour better than a new standalone AI app. Planera's MCP connector suggests a competitor is moving the same way.
- Acceptable AI: advisory functions such as risk forecasting on existing schedules, schedule QA (DCMA-style checks), and linking RFIs and submittals to activities. Distrusted: AI that rewrites logic or durations without review. Explainability, audit trails and "propose, don't apply" workflows answer the trust barrier directly.
- nPlan's displacement of QSRA at TRU (vendor claim) suggests that risk forecasting from historical data has the most credible adoption path, especially in UK rail and infrastructure.
- Generative sequencing (ALICE) needs structured or BIM inputs, which is a barrier. That supports not making BIM a prerequisite in our product.

### Gaps
- No independent, third-party adoption study or audit of nPlan or ALICE outcomes was found.
- No reliable 2024–2026 reporting on ALICE's commercial status (customers, layoffs, pivots) was found.
- Nodes & Links, SmartPM and other schedule-QA or analytics entrants were not covered. Searches returned nothing on them.
- No practitioner-level (Reddit or LinkedIn) statements on natural-language schedule editing were found.

## 4. Pricing/licensing complaints and adoption barriers for these tools

### Takeaway
Pricing complaints centre on opaque, volume-based or per-user pricing and on rises at renewal. For specialist tools the barriers are learning curve, data rigour (structured or BIM inputs) and the contractual lock-in to P6 deliverables, which forces teams to pay for two tools.

### Cited Findings
- Procore pricing is custom and based on annual construction volume, which makes it expensive for smaller firms (frequency High across review roundups). — [Connecteam Procore review](https://connecteam.com/reviews/procore/); [G2 Procore](https://www.g2.com/products/procore/reviews)
- Buildertrend: price is not published. A Capterra user reports a large price increase without notice, and GetOneCrew lists price rises at renewal among the most common complaints. — [Fervor Studio](https://fervorstudio.ca/news/buildertrend-review-pricing-alternatives/); [Capterra Buildertrend](https://www.capterra.com/p/70092/Buildertrend/reviews/); [GetOneCrew](https://www.getonecrew.com/post/buildertrend-reviews)
- Smartsheet: per-user pricing adds up for large teams, and advanced features have a steep learning curve. — [Buildern](https://buildern.com/resources/blog/construction-scheduling-software/); [TeamGantt construction](https://www.teamgantt.com/construction/construction-scheduling-software)
- Synchro: a high entry price point, and learning the tool is overwhelming for occasional users. — [Capterra Synchro](https://www.capterra.com/p/35289/Synchro/reviews/)
- ALICE: requires BIM or structured parametric inputs (adoption barrier) and has a learning curve. — [AI Home Building](https://aihomebuilding.com/articles/ai-scheduling-garbage-data); [ai-review.com](https://ai-review.com/business/alice-technologies/)
- Dual-tool cost: teams keep P6 for owner submittals and add Touchplan, Outbuild or Procore for field and lookahead work. — [Outbuild blog](https://www.outbuild.com/blog/best-construction-scheduling-software); [Guideflow](https://www.guideflow.com/blog/construction-scheduling-software)
- In the Mastt 2026 survey, cost fell to 3.7% as an AI barrier (from 18.8% in 2025) and data privacy to 8.3% (from 25.7%), while trust and accuracy rose to the top. — [Mastt 2026](https://www.mastt.com/research/ai-in-construction-project-management-2026)

### Inferences
- Why customers choose these tools: field adoption, an all-in-one platform (Procore, Buildertrend), visual Last Planner workflows (Touchplan, Outbuild), visual 4D communication (Synchro, relevant only for buyer choice), and linear-work fit (Tilos). Why they stay on or return to P6 or MS Project: CPM depth, baselines, XER contract deliverables.
- A low-friction, standards-based layer on top of existing P6, Asta or MSP files that adds collaboration, QA and AI without forcing a second licensed scheduling platform would answer both the dual-tool cost complaint and the trust barrier.

### Gaps
- No published list prices were found for nPlan, ALICE, Touchplan, Outbuild or Tilos. BuiltStack's ALICE vs nPlan guide claims to cover pricing but was not fetched.
- No churn or "why we left" testimonials were found for ALICE, nPlan or Outbuild.
