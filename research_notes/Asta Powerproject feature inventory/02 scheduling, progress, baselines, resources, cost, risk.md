## Ten features the documentation presents as Powerproject's signature strengths in these areas

1. **Unlimited baselines with embedded storage, merge, revert and "jagged progress" comparison** — the features page leads with unlimited baselines as litigation evidence; the help adds merging, what-if revert, project baselines and the Manhattan-skyline view.
2. **Multiple progress periods with per-period coloured shading and straight/jagged progress lines** — the help's core progress model; marketed as proof of when work actually happened and whether tasks are ahead or behind.
3. **Schedule Quality Check with pre-configured DCMA 14-point and CIOB PP21 metric sets** — introduced in v15 and promoted on the features page as compliance checking against industry or company standards.
4. **Built-in Risk Analysis (Monte Carlo / Latin Hypercube) with sensitivity, criticality and finish-date/cost likelihood reports** — the features page calls it a built-in tool for flagging risky tasks and estimating probability of hitting a date or budget.
5. **Buffer tasks and NEC support (terminal float, time-risk allowance fields, feeder/project buffers with consumption tracking)** — a construction-specific scheduling capability given its own help section and template content.
6. **Deep reschedule control: out-of-sequence options, critical path integrity test, critical path drag, cascade numbering, logical float paths and delivery-date deadlines** — the rescheduling section is the largest scheduling topic and these are presented as analytical differentiators.
7. **Resource management across a programme: demand vs scheduled allocations, skills, shift-pattern calendars, work rates/overtime, availability checks and a leveller that delays, splits or stretches** — the features page's "Manage resources across your programme" claim, detailed in the resources help.
8. **Cost control with cost centres, time-typed rates, income, histogram S-curves and earned value (SPI/CPI/EAC)** — "Maintain visibility of costs" on the features page; EVA fields and S-curve export documented in the help.
9. **Flexible calendars (work patterns + exceptions) applied per task and per resource, with lag counted on predecessor/successor calendars or elapsed time** — the calendar model underpins the shift and overtime claims on the features page.
10. **Compact bar-chart modelling: several tasks per bar with implied links, task work/work-rate modelling with materials, task activities and what-if scenarios via baselines** — "Adaptable layouts to suit your style of planning" and "scenario simulations" on the features page, documented in the bar-chart editing and modelling topics.
