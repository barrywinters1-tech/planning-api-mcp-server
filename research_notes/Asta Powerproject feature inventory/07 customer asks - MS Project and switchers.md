# 07 — Customer asks: Microsoft Project (desktop, Project Online, Project for the web / Planner Premium) and people who switched to simpler tools

Compiled 2026-11-21. Read-only research; nothing here is verified against the products themselves beyond what the cited pages say.

## 1. Method note

**Question.** What have Microsoft Project customers asked for, complained about or worked around for years without Microsoft fixing it — with particular attention to construction users and to people who left MS Project, Asta Powerproject or Primavera P6 for simpler tools (Smartsheet, monday.com, Procore scheduling, Buildertrend, Fieldwire, TeamGantt, Excel) and said why.

**Searches.** 44 distinct web searches (English, US index) across: Microsoft Feedback Portal / UserVoice references, Microsoft Tech Community (Project and Planner forums), Microsoft Q&A / Answers, MPUG, review aggregators (G2, Capterra, Software Advice, TrustRadius, PeerSpot/IT Central Station, Gartner Peer Insights, GetApp, SelectHub), Planning Planet / Project Controls forums, projectmanagement.com, Quarter to Three, Fine Homebuilding, construction trade press (ForConstructionPros, Construction Dive, Bricks & Bytes), and vendor comparison pages (Smartsheet, Outbuild, Planera, Sitedrive, Celoxis, ClickUp, ProjectManager.com, TeamGantt, Procore help centre, Oracle KB).

**Fetches.** About 50 pages fetched in full and read. Roughly 15 more returned 403 or 404 to the crawler.

**Access limits (affects counts).** reddit.com is explicitly blocked to this crawler (the search API refused the domain), and `site:reddit.com` queries returned no Reddit pages. LinkedIn, YouTube comments, G2 review pages, Capterra review pages, Software Advice, Gartner Peer Insights, Smartsheet Community, MPUG forum threads and projectmanagement.com threads returned 403. For those sources I relied on (a) dated excerpts surfaced in search result snippets, (b) a GummySearch aggregate of 206 Reddit reviews across 12 subreddits (158 from r/projectmanagement), and (c) secondary articles that quote reviews. Where a count below says "snippet only", the quote was seen in a search snippet, not the page. Reddit evidence is therefore thinner than the brief asked for; the themes it would have added (licensing confusion, "overkill for small teams", Planner replacing Project) are well covered by Tech Community and review-site sources instead.

**Vendor bias.** Outbuild, Planera, Sitedrive, Smartsheet, Celoxis, ClickUp and ProjectManager.com publish comparison content that sells against MS Project or P6. I use their *named customer quotes* (name, role, company) as evidence of switcher sentiment, and their unattributed claims only as "vendor claim". Each such row is marked (V).

**Counting rule.** "Sources" = number of distinct pages/threads/reviews in which the ask appears, as found in this exercise. It is a lower bound. "Earliest" = the earliest dated instance I saw, not the earliest that exists.

**Status key.** Unaddressed = no Microsoft (or relevant vendor) change found. Partly = delivered in a reduced form, or only in one of the product lines, or then retired. Addressed = delivered, with version/date.

**Product key.** Desktop = Project Standard/Professional/Project Online Desktop Client (.mpp). PO = Project Online / Project Web App (retired 30 Sep 2026). P4W = Project for the web (retired Aug 2025, redirected to Planner). PP = Planner Premium (Plan 1/3/5 inside the new Planner). P6 = Oracle Primavera P6. Asta = Asta Powerproject.

**Dates that frame everything.** P4W released 2019; critical path added to P4W Oct 2021; guest access added Apr 2022; project-level baseline in P4W May 2024 (task-level still missing Jun 2024); P4W retired into Planner Aug 2025; Project Online end-of-sale 1 Oct 2025, retired 30 Sep 2026 (users reported access ending on or a few days after 1 Oct 2026). Project 2024 perpetual desktop released Oct 2024.

**Patterns in why they left.** (1) Cost per seat versus how many people need to *see* the plan — the licence-per-viewer problem (C1) is the most common trigger. (2) Collaboration: single-writer .mpp files and F5-refresh P6 versus browser co-editing. (3) Learning curve for occasional and field users; superintendents in particular. (4) Microsoft's own churn: P4W customers were moved to Planner and several say they went to Excel or a third party rather than follow. (5) Mac. (6) The lookahead workflow in construction — the schedule lives in MSP but the work happens in Excel.

### 4.2 What they miss after leaving (the simple tools' gaps)

- **Scheduling engine depth.** Smartsheet users ask for "more features from MS Project to help with making dependencies more formula driven", resource levelling ("pretty useless" without it), and critical path/slack in list view (L1, L4). monday.com is finish-to-start only with critical path gated or in trial (L5). Fieldwire has no dependencies at all (L8). Buildertrend lacks FF/SF and users report cascades that "do not always flow through" (L7). TeamGantt users want critical-path indicators and better resource management (L6).
- **Baselines and reporting.** Smartsheet baseline bars do not appear in reports, dashboards or print (L2); TeamGantt baselines are tier-gated; Procore's integrated schedule is read-only apart from % complete (E9).
- **Import fidelity.** Smartsheet's .mpp import "ended their trial" for one evaluator; Excel round-trips lose dates or add constraints (E3, E10).
- **Hidden extra cost.** Smartsheet Resource Management is a separate licence ("never able to use" it); monday critical path needs Pro/Enterprise; Fieldwire Basic caps projects (L3, L5).
- **Scale.** Fieldwire Basic caps users and projects; Buildertrend has no master schedule across jobs; Excel breaks past a 3–4 week lookahead (L7, L14).
- **Contractual acceptability.** Public and owner-driven work still requires native P6/XER or .mpp submissions and float/retained-logic analysis for claims, so the "simple" tool ends up alongside, not instead of, the heavyweight (L13). The common end-state is a hybrid: one scheduler keeps MSP/P6 as the record; the field works in Procore, Excel or a lookahead tool.
- **What P6 defenders say they would lose** (Project Controls 2011): accurate float after levelling, lag calendars, shift work, retained logic — none of which the simple tools attempt (H2, E11, G3).

### 4.3 What would bring them back (or keep them)

Inferred from what switchers praised in the new tool and what stayers defend, not from stated "I'd return if" quotes (none found):

1. **Viewing without a licence** — a free, live, read-only link (or a cheap viewer seat) is the gap every substitute filled first (Smartsheet, TeamGantt, GanttPRO, Planera, Outbuild all lead with it).
2. **A CPM engine with a field-grade front end** — drag-and-drop, change-one-date-and-everything-updates, iPad updates on site, with the desktop's dependency types, calendars and float underneath (Garney, Miles-McClellan, Harkins quotes).
3. **Lookahead and master schedule in one place**, with a change log and reasons (Outbuild quotes; G4, D2).
4. **One understandable price, one product name**, and a roadmap that holds for five years (B2, B4, J3).
5. **Mac and browser parity** with the desktop engine (E4, L12).
6. **.mpp/XER fidelity** so firms on owner-mandated formats are not forced to run two tools (E1, L13).
7. **Printable one-page Gantts and client-ready overlays** out of the box (F1, F2).
